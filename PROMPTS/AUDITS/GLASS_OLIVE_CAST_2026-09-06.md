# AUDIT — the olive/yellow cast on crazed glass panes near a blast

**Status:** ✅ **CLOSED 2026-09-06** — root named, fixed, measured red-to-green, pinned
by a selftest with teeth. §7 below is the answer; §1–§5 are kept verbatim as the
hunt's record (§5's three leads were all wrong, and it is worth knowing why).
**Opened:** 2026-09-06. Director asked to stop and write the state down; continued
the same day.
**Supersedes** the diagnosis in `GLASS_MASTER_PLAN.md` §18.14b (commit `03444a61`),

## 1. The symptom

A glass pane that goes **CRACKED** from a nearby grenade blast reads **olive‑green /
yellow** on the perspective view the blast happened in. It is stable (does not fade
meaningfully over 4 s / 240 frames), and it **vanishes completely on a perspective
rotation**, leaving the pane its normal blue‑grey.

- Director‑reported 2026‑09‑06, and **pre‑existing** — he has seen it "várias vezes".
  **Not** introduced by the G4‑4 shard‑rain work.
- The FLOOR‑CRATER‑01 fix (commits `921a5009` / `5cb1d948`, the magenta canary) removed
  a *different* bug — the flat orange floor placeholder showing through a rotated
  crater — and did **not** touch this olive cast.

### Isolation results

1. **dev vision OFF** → olive barely moves (`20002 → 17187` matching px; pane pixel
   `(115,133,47) → (106,122,45)`). **Not a dev/HUD overlay.**
2. **hide the craze sprite** (`_glass_crack_root`), panes visible → olive *increases*
   (`→ 25874`). The whitish craze web was slightly veiling the olive, not causing it.
3. **hide the glass panes** → ~75 % of the olive gone; what remains is the pure
   yellow `(164,164,5)` behind. **The pane composite tints that yellow → olive.**
4. **hide `_glass_backbuffer`** (the `BackBufferCopy`) → **no change** to the pane
   pixel (`(115,133,48)`). The pane is **not** picking the yellow up from
   `glass_screen` / `SCREEN_UV` via the BackBufferCopy node. (Godot still resolves
   `hint_screen_texture` without the node; and the yellow is directly behind the pane
   in screen space regardless.)
5. **force `crack_color` red** → proves the yellow FILL is not the craze shader output.
6. **node sweep** (`self` + `_voxel_renderer` + `VisionController` children, hide one
   at a time): only hiding the **entire `_voxel_renderer`** kills the yellow (`n=15`).
   No single `voxel_layer_72..103`, `glass_backbuffer`, or `glass_crack_root` removes
   it. Biggest single drop is `voxel_layer_79` (FLOOR_TOP). ⚠️ **The sweep is
   confounded** — it takes ~60 frames and the transient VFX overlays (smoke / spark /
   ember, z 37‑39) decay across it, so `smoke_spark_overlay`'s apparent large drop is
   not trustworthy.

`crack_field_origin`/`dir`/`pane_lo`/`pane_hi`/`crack_occupancy`/`crack_opening`

## 4. What §18.14b got wrong

§18.14b (from `INFILTRAITOR_GLASS_WARM_PROBE`, commit `03444a61`) claimed the cast is
**"warm blast content in the framebuffer sampled via `SCREEN_UV`"**, rooted in the
**G‑D18b z‑bump** putting the glass composite over a floor crater. This session:

- Hiding the `BackBufferCopy` changes the pane pixel **not at all** → the SCREEN_UV /
  backbuffer path is not how the yellow reaches the pane.
- The cast is **stable for 4 s**, not a fading warm ramp.
- The **floor crater renders correct dark soot** in the same frame (FLOOR‑CRATER‑01
  is fixed) — the yellow is *not* the crater.
- The crazed panes are plain `glass`, and the yellow behind them is a clean
  pane‑shaped slab — consistent with a wall/pane‑anchored effect, not a floor one.

The `SCREEN_UV` note may still be partly right (the pane clearly shows *something*
behind it), but the "warm blast content / crater in the backbuffer" story is not it.

## 5. Open leads for next session

1. **Name the pure yellow `(164,164,5)` slab.** It is pane‑shaped, in the
   `_voxel_renderer` subtree, blast‑view only, cleared by rotation. Bisect cleanly:
   hide opaque `voxel_layer_80/81/82` **and** `_glass_layers[80/81/82]` individually,
   with a fresh control between each and VFX frozen or long‑decayed (wait ≥400
   frames, or gate the VFX overlays off up front) so the decay confound is gone.
2. **Rotation clears it → it is applied at render time during the blast and not
   replayed on rebuild.** That points at a *transient* effect, not persisted damage
   state. Check `play_consequence_light()` and the VL light‑field **alt minting** for
   a non‑greyscale / warm modulate on the wall the pane sits in — `_ensure_light_alt`
   is supposed to be `Color(lum,lum,lum,soot_alpha)` (greyscale), so a warm alt there
   would be the bug.
3. **The atom BLUE channel (`t.b`) is the tint index `glass_class_tint()` reads.**
   Even though the crazed panes are plain `glass`, verify no CRACK / craze path
   writes a *variant* tint index (green=2 `(0.24,0.62,0.34)`, amber=4
   `(0.82,0.60,0.20)`) into a plain‑glass voxel's atom. `glass_screen_amber` tint is
   very close to the observed yellow.
4. Consider whether the yellow is the **craze OPENING mask or occupancy image**
   bound as a shader texture being *drawn* somewhere (an `ImageTexture` rendered
   raw), or a `_glass_rim` source.

## 7. THE ANSWER (2026-09-06, same day)

### 7.1 What it was

**`DetonationPlanBuilder._resolve_damaged_tile()` stamped GLASS ATOMS onto the
OPAQUE voxel layer.**

`VoxelRenderer._set_voxel_cell()` has two modes. With `apply = true` it PLACES the
cell and its glass branch is careful: glass goes to `_glass_layers[level]` and the
opaque cell is explicitly erased. With `apply = false` it only RESOLVES — it hands
back a source id and the caller places it — and that branch returned the glass pane
atom's id with **nothing in the returned dict to say which layer it belongs to**.
Every consumer of that seam assumed "the opaque layer", because the dict gave it no
way to ask.

`DetonationEntryWriter.apply()` writes the `dented` and `cracked` waves to
`voxel_renderer.get_layer(level)` — the opaque layer. So the chain was:

1. the blast crazes a glass voxel → `delta.state_of(voxel) == CRACKED`;
2. `_resolve_damaged_tile()` misses the baked registry (glass has no damage
   variants — D22/G-D26, and `damage_variant_material()` returns the BASE glass name
   for every state precisely so a crazed pane does not change material);
3. it falls to the live resolve, `_set_voxel_cell(…, apply = false)`, which returns
   the pane's own atom id;
4. `DetonationEntryWriter` stamps that id on the **opaque** layer, underneath the
   still-intact pane.

A glass atom's RGB is `(dim, dim, tint_index / 255)` — an instruction to
`glass_pane.gdshader`, which reads R as the per-plane dim and B as the family tint
index. `voxel_face_shading.gdshader` reads none of that and renders the texture
literally: **flat yellow**, R == G, B ≈ 0. The real pane then MULTIPLIED
`PANE_TINT[0]` (`0.47, 0.63, 0.90`, blue) over it — **olive**.

### 7.2 Every observation in §3, explained

| observation | why |
|---|---|
| pure yellow `(164,164,5)` behind the pane | the glass atom's own RGB, `(1, 1, 0)` for plain glass, darkened by bucket × soot |
| exactly `(101,101,2)` / `(103,103,1)` with the craze hidden | `voxel_face_shading`'s **FACE-READ-03 residue snap** forces each face onto its own class mod 3 of the 8-bit value: blue 0 → **2** on the SW face, **1** on SE. Both panes are `_SW`. The blue channel was never a tint index — it was the residue correction |
| the pane MULTIPLY-tints it olive | correct shader on wrong input, exactly as §3 concluded |
| the `BackBufferCopy` changes nothing | the yellow is a real tile in the scene, not framebuffer content |
| stable for 4 s, not a fading ramp | a placed cell, not a transient effect |
| **no single `voxel_layer_N` removes it** | the panes span **24 levels (80..103)**; hiding one layer removes one row of a 24-row wall |
| only hiding the whole `_voxel_renderer` kills it | all 24 of those layers are its children |
| a rotation clears it | the rebuild re-runs `_set_voxel_cell(apply = true)`, whose glass branch calls `layer.erase_cell()` |
| the opaque cell probed EMPTY at `(88,87)` L80 | L80 held 24 strays out of 720; that one cell was not among them |

### 7.3 Why §5's three leads were all wrong

They were all hypotheses about a **colour being computed**. The defect was a
**correct texture on the wrong layer** — no colour was ever computed wrongly. The
light-alt path is greyscale as documented; no variant tint index was mis-written;
no mask `ImageTexture` was drawn raw. Worth keeping: the session that wrote them had
already measured everything needed to find this, and read `(101,101,2)` as a colour
to explain rather than as a **signature to match against something the codebase
prints**. `_build_glass_pane_atom()` writes `Color(rgb, rgb, tint_b, a)` — literally
that signature, in the file.

### 7.4 The fix

One authority, asked rather than re-derived:

- `_set_voxel_cell()`'s resolve-only glass return gained **`"glass_sublayer": true`**
  — the routing rule stays in the one place that owns it;
- `_resolve_damaged_tile()` returns **`{}`** on all three of its live-resolve
  branches (Slice, JunctionColumn, Slab) when the resolve is so marked. Empty is a
  real answer here — *"this voxel has no opaque tile"* — not a miss;
- the plan builder skips only the **tile entry**. The damage is still recorded
  (`touched_voxels` is the commit's persistence seam and VL-PERSIST replays a CRACKED
  pane through a rotation from it), and the craze web — a sprite over the pane — is
  the entire visual for a cracked pane exactly as G-D27 ratified.

Re-deriving the rule at the call site from `GlassMaterials.is_glass()` was rejected:
it would also have to re-derive `flat_baked` and `_glass_atom_source`, which is three
copies of one decision — the [[two-authorities-remove-one]] trap this track has paid
for before.
